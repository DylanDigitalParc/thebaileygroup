What the Gaston Award Recognizes

The Gaston Award is ICF Minnesota's honor for selfless leadership and sustained contribution to the coaching profession and the chapter. Recipients must:

  • Hold a qualifying coaching credential
  • Demonstrate clear leadership in advancing the profession
  • Show integrity and ethical service
  • Be an active ICF Minnesota member for at least five years — with meaningful contributions such as board service, volunteering on chapter initiatives, or mentoring fellow coaches

It's a recognition built on years of consistent give-back to the coaching community, not a single achievement.

As Ann has described her path to the firm, joining The Bailey Group in 2021 as an executive coach and being asked to lead the organization as CEO in 2025 has been the natural next step in a career devoted to developing people.

Our client partners

Our Clients, Recognized with the ICF Minnesota PRISM Award

The PRISM Award isn't a popularity contest. It verifies a coaching program's real impact — whether that's stronger leaders, a shift in organizational culture, or measurable outcomes like retention and engagement. It's a recognition earned through evidence, not just intent.

How a PRISM Award is earned

  1. NominatedBy a credentialed coach.
  2. Active coachingThe organization actively used coaching in the year before nomination.
  3. Verified impactAn interview with ICF Minnesota's Selection Committee confirms the program's results.
  4. Live showcaseFinalists present their coaching story at the ICF Minnesota Awards Showcase before a winner is named.

2024 PRISM Award

Braun Intertec

Braun's leadership cohort combined group coaching, individual coaching, and facilitated learning to accelerate development among key leaders across the organization's diverse geographic and functional lines. The impact reached well beyond individual growth. Leaders who rarely interacted built trust and learned to collaborate across business lines — capability that becomes essential as they take on broader roles.

That trust proved its value during a significant leadership transition, when the cohort created space for candid, constructive dialogue between participants and new executive leadership — the kind of open exchange the client described as unlike anything seen before.
